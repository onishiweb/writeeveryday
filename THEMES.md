# Prompt Themes Analysis

Analysis of all 365 prompts (January–December) grouped by theme, with gaps identified and future theme suggestions.

## Existing Themes

### 1. Single-word / Abstract Themes (89 prompts)
Completion, laziness, loneliness, motivation, anxiety, adventure, chaos, warmth, blue, companionship, exploration, quiet, green, encounter, distance, rain, gold, grey, artificial, weight, silence, absence, belonging, shelter, home, return, invisible, skin, breath, pulse, hunger, fever, need, numb, anonymous, recognition, ordinary, unfamiliar, wilderness, wild, growth, migration, drought, bloom, roots, moonlight, ambition, burnout, promotion, grind, legacy, reinvention, craft, purpose, pride, jealousy, shame, obsession, nostalgia, faith, loss, dread, dark, omen, threshold, mask, hollow, warning, whisper, changeling, dust, forgetting, archive, remembrance, rust, inheritance, patience, ruins, time, light, generosity, candlelight, forgiveness, snow, hope, gathering, midnight, resolution, beginning

> Still the backbone of the project — now a quarter of the year. Consistent, memorable, endlessly reinterpretable, and with no word repeated across all 365 days.

### 2. Genre Fiction Exercises (26 prompts)
- **Fantasy** (9): discovering a power, first-person world-building, a weapon sales pitch, magic going wrong, something ordinary turning out to be magical, a living forest, training under a demanding master, remembering a world nobody else does, deep winter
- **Crime** (5): crime discovered, killer revealed, a witness who knows nothing, a workplace, the detective afraid
- **Sci-fi** (7): an opening scene, technology failing, someone who has never seen Earth, inhabiting a body that isn't theirs, nature reclaiming a city, a job now done by a machine, a memory that can be bought
- **Horror** (4): the haunted house, physical sensation rather than fear, the woods at night, the monster's point of view
- **Romance** (1): noticing a love interest

> Fantasy and sci-fi are now well served across the year. Romance remains the thinnest genre by a wide margin — one prompt in 365.

### 3. Alternative Formats (~18 prompts)
Recipe, positive review, negative review, app review, screenplay dialogue, letter to past self, complaint letter, comedic to-do list, a list that isn't a list, letter never sent, property listing, nature documentary narration, resignation letter, performance review, ghost story under 200 words, modern retelling of a legend, cautionary tale for children, final diary entry

> A real strength, and one that grew steadily through the year — the autumn months added the folk and documentary registers to the existing commercial ones.

### 4. Emotional States & Psychology (~30 prompts)
Loneliness, anxiety, grief, motivation, laziness, waiting, exhaustion, procrastination, internal debate, making it through, pride, shame, jealousy, obsession, need, numbness, burnout, nostalgia, dread, fear of being found out, a phobia from the inside, bravery nobody notices, hollowness, patience, forgiveness, hope, a promise meant this time, taking stock, what someone becomes unwatched

> No longer anxiety-dominated. The joy–pride–shame–jealousy gap identified last time is filled, and October added fear as a subject in its own right rather than a genre effect.

### 5. Relationships & Human Connection (~30 prompts)
Argument between friends, reconnecting, teaching, betrayal, companionship, unwanted gift, knowing something you shouldn't, being lied to, both people right, strangers stuck together, overheard conversation, a kindness from someone unnamed, colleagues becoming rivals, a mentor, siblings dividing belongings, a grandparent and grandchild with nothing in common, a meal between people who shouldn't share a table, letting a friendship end, a goodbye that went well, coming home after years

> The strongest category in the set, and the one most improved by July, November and December. Good spread across conflict, tenderness, obligation and release.

### 6. Craft & Technique Prompts (~14 prompts)
Scene with a twist, flashback, happy ending that feels wrong, told entirely through objects, death of a hero/villain, dialogue only, something loud, something very short, no dialogue outdoors, a background character's perspective, the monster's viewpoint, told through what a character can feel but not see, a scene told entirely in a kitchen, under-200-words constraint

> Still the most valuable prompts for developing writers, and the constraint-based ones ("no dialogue", "under 200 words", "entirely through objects") are the most distinctive thing the project does.

### 7. Sensory Prompts (~13 prompts)
A specific smell, something that doesn't taste good, the first taste of something extraordinary, physical exhaustion, warmth, something loud, silence, a sound that means you're home, skin, awareness of hands, breath, too hot or too cold, the smell after rain, the first frost, fog

> Roughly doubled by June and August. Taste is now the under-served sense — two prompts out of 365.

### 8. Everyday Life & Mundane Settings (~15 prompts)
Dentist waiting room, breakfast, car salesman, interview prep, restaurant, email after a meeting, public transport, an airport or station, an office after hours, the end of a long shift, a museum after closing, a queue, a room unchanged in decades, a cellar or attic, a kitchen during a celebration

> Still some of the best prompts here. The year added a lot of *institutional* mundanity — workplaces, waiting rooms, transit — which suits the genre-neutral writer.

### 9. Character Work (~10 prompts)
Character backstory, villain's origin story, day in the life of a cat/dog/bird, a performance review for a fictional character, someone excellent at a job they hate, someone who talks to plants, someone who writes everything down, someone who works alone

> Improved but still thin relative to how foundational character is. Most character prompts arrive indirectly, through situation rather than interiority.

### 10. Humour & Satire (~6 prompts)
Comedic to-do list, negative review, fantasy weapon sales pitch, email that didn't need to be sent, property listing for an unsellable house, nature documentary narration for something ordinary

> The thinnest vein in the project, and it thins further after September — the final quarter is almost entirely serious in register. Worth correcting in a future cycle.

### 11. Journey & Transition (~12 prompts)
Embarking on a journey, travelling together, celebrating something new, making it through, moving house, returning to a place from the past, a river changing course, starting over in a career, the last day at a job someone loved, leaving a party early, leaving something behind, the person you'll be a year from now

> Now one of the better-developed categories, and it gives the year its shape — the arc from January's beginnings to December's endings and beginnings is the clearest piece of curation in the set.

## Gaps in the Current Prompts

- ~~Very few prompts about **family** or intergenerational relationships~~ — **addressed** by November (Memory & Family), with April's family tradition prompt as a precursor
- ~~No prompts focused on **place** as a primary subject~~ — **addressed** by May (Home) and August (Nature); April's "a setting that tells the story of that place" is the clearest single example
- ~~No **mythology, folklore, or fairy tale** exercises~~ — **addressed** by October (Fear & Folklore), plus April's myth/folktale and subverted fairy tale
- ~~No prompts about **childhood or memory** specifically~~ — **addressed** by November, plus April's recurring childhood memory
- **Class, money, and social dynamics** — only partially addressed. September covers labour and ambition and April has "a scene where money fixes everything", but money, debt and class are still close to absent as subjects
- ~~Light on **nature and the physical world**~~ — **addressed** by August (Nature)
- ~~Almost no **body-focused** writing (health, illness, physicality)~~ — **addressed** by June (The Body), though illness and recovery specifically remain untouched
- ~~No **moral dilemma** prompts beyond the genie wish~~ — **addressed** by April (Power), including "a moral dilemma with no good answer" and "a scene where someone refuses an order"

Two gaps have opened up over the year that weren't visible in July:

- **Humour** is now badly under-weighted in the second half — the last four months contain no comic prompts at all
- **Romance** has a single prompt in the entire year, despite February being themed around companionship and love

## Suggested Future Themes

The list below drops everything the year now covers and keeps only what's still open, plus a few additions suggested by how the first full cycle turned out.

| Theme | Why it's valuable |
|---|---|
| **Money** | Class, aspiration, desperation — still the biggest hole in the set |
| **Illness & Recovery** | Vulnerability, dependency, the body as subject — June covered sensation but not health |
| **Failure** | Specific, underwritten — distinct from grief or loss |
| **Secrets** | A few secret-adjacent prompts exist; the theme has much more range |
| **Technology** | AI, surveillance, devices — increasingly universal human experience |
| **Comedy** *(as a month)* | The thinnest vein in the project; a dedicated month would force the register |
| **Desire** | Romance is one prompt in 365 — a month of attraction, courtship and refusal |
| **Justice** | Law, revenge, fairness, who gets believed |
| **Food & Appetite** | Taste is the least-used sense; cooking and eating are social as well as sensory |
| **Neighbours** | Proximity without intimacy — a different problem from July's strangers |
| **Sleep & Dreams** | Licenses surrealism, which the set currently has almost none of |
| **Silence** *(expanded)* | A whole month of silence variations could still work as a dedicated theme |

## Monthly Themes

One theme per month gives writers a sense of arc across the year and makes the prompt set feel curated rather than random. The full cycle:

| Month | Theme |
|---|---|
| January | New Beginnings |
| February | Companionship & Love |
| March | Weight & Consequence |
| April | Power |
| May | Home |
| June | The Body |
| July | Strangers |
| August | Nature |
| September | Work & Ambition |
| October | Fear & Folklore |
| November | Memory & Family |
| December | Endings & Beginnings |

April sits slightly apart from the others: rather than developing one theme, it samples many — myth, money, nature, work, the body, moral dilemma — several of which later became months of their own.
